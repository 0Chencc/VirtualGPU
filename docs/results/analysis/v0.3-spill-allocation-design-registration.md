# spill-latency: reading the reload points across local memory allocations (registered design, draft)

Status: draft, registered before any implementation or run; to be reviewed and approved before either. Nothing in the design or the adoption check changes after the adoption capture is taken.

## Why

In the first GB10 0.23.0 capture (main `42e039d`), the eight 4 KiB `l2_spill` points with reloads failed rule 3: their round medians spread 24% to 31%, every point in the same order of rounds. The allocation diagnostic ([gb10-spill-alloc-plan.md](gb10-spill-alloc-plan.md), branch `s8/diag-spill-alloc`, result `f8e0ff8`) found the cause: the level of these points is set by the local memory allocation the frames live in and stays fixed while it lives (18 of 18 rereads of one allocation within 0.008, 18 of 18 allocations stable), while a new allocation draws a new level (spans 0.23 to 0.35 within a process). A process normally keeps one allocation for all its launches, so each round of the capture had one level. The points without reloads do not move (spans at most 0.006). The same note found that the stack limit itself changes some points (the batched 512 B point at 128 reloads takes 1.75 ms at 4 and 5 KiB of stack and 1.29 ms at 6 KiB and at the default), so new allocations here come from new contexts at the default stack limit, not from stack-limit raises.

This is a change of how the points are measured, not a device exception and not a removal of points.

## Design

- Each `spill-latency` process reads every point with reloads (all six frames, both dependency modes, 8, 32, 68 and 128 reloads: 48 points) in N local memory allocations, N = 15, each in a new context (`cudaDeviceReset`) at the default stack limit, at the frozen steps of the point, 15 samples and 3 warm-ups per allocation, in the usual point order within an allocation. The first allocation is the process's first context.
- A point's sample j is the mean over the N allocations of their j-th samples (launch times), so each point still has 15 samples for rules 1 to 3 and `long_capture.py`. Every allocation's samples are kept in the raw output (`allocation_launch_ns`, a list of N lists, named without the `_samples` suffix so that the merge and `profile_launch.py` do not take it for a value), with the allocation's context index.
- Each point also records, as a description that no rule judges, the span of its N per-allocation medians over their median.
- The points without reloads are read once, in the first allocation, as now.
- In the profile, a point's `rel_uncertainty` is at least half the span of its per-allocation medians over the valid rounds, divided by its value, so that it covers the allocation levels.
- One method for every point with reloads, so that no frame is a special case: the 4 KiB frames need it, and the small frames lose nothing by it (at an unchanged stack limit the batched 512 B point at 128 reloads differed by at most 1.4% between allocations).

## Why the mean and N = 15

The brief asked for the median over allocations. On the diagnostic's 18 allocation levels (resampled, five processes, rule 3 passing when the five process values span at most 10%), the median over N allocations reaches a 0.96 pass probability only at N = 45, because the levels come in clusters and the median of a small sample jumps between them; the mean reaches 0.93 at N = 12 and 0.97 at N = 15 (with the 18 levels themselves resampled, the 10th percentile of the pass probability at N = 12 is 0.85). The mean of N allocations is used, with N = 15 fixed here. A single slow launch moves the mean of its sample position by 1/15 of its excess.

Two limits of this basis: the 18 levels come from allocations made partly by stack-limit raises, which also change the stride between frames; and only 6 of them come from new contexts at an unchanged stride. The adoption check below is therefore the test of the choice, and N is not changed after it.

## The points without reloads and the launch cost of PR #19

The points without reloads stay as they are because they do not move with the allocation (spans 0.004 to 0.007 at 4 KiB and 0.003 to 0.005 at 512 B). `extra_ns_vs_zero` then subtracts a single-allocation zero point from an allocation-averaged point, which is consistent because the zero point is the same in every allocation. The launches with no steps of PR #19 time the per-launch frame cost, which is the cost the zero point carries; it does not move with the allocation either, so the cost taken out per step is unchanged.

## The serial 1 KiB points

The four serial-chain points at 1 KiB frames with reloads take one of two states per launch within one allocation. They are not the four `l1_boundary` points of the registered spill decision (`ad1d505`, test 2 uses the batched 1 KiB points), but that registration forbids dropping points to make a GPU eligible, so they are kept and measured by the same method. Treating the capture's seven rounds as seven allocations, the mean of 15 allocations would keep rule 2 from flagging them in most rounds (0.1% to 18% per point) but would still flag rule 1 at 26% to 73% per point; whether their states follow the allocation is not known. The adoption check counts all 60 points (rule 1 and rule 3 allow 3 failures, rule 2 allows 9). The earlier offline account of 8 failing points out of 56 (four serial points left out, rule 3) belongs to the old single-allocation measurement and is not used here.

## Adoption check

- GB10, a long-launch capture of `spill-latency` alone with `microbench/long_capture.py`: pilot and 5 rounds, with the usual gates.
- Adopted when all 5 rounds are valid on rules 1 and 2 and rule 3 passes across them, with no extra round used beyond the protocol's limit of 2. Otherwise the design is withdrawn and the mode stays as it is; N is not changed and the check is not repeated with another N.
- Reported either way: every point's per-allocation spans, the rule counts per round, and whether a failure comes from the serial 1 KiB points, the 4 KiB points or others.

## Limits

This design reports the level averaged over local memory allocations. A real kernel runs in one allocation and lands on one level: on GB10 the 4 KiB reload points moved by about +/-15% around the mean (level standard deviation 0.10 over 18 allocations). Whether the core states this as a limit of the reload latency (design.md 9.2) is decided by the core owner. The design does not explain why the level depends on the allocation, nor the stride effect of the stack limit, which a kernel with a larger stack would meet.
