## Capture and qualification

Seven of the 20 scheduled independent processes completed between
2026-11-26 23:11:45 and 23:34:29 UTC. All seven had 15 samples per point,
a passing thermal gate and a shortest launch of at least 1 ms. The second
L2 process exited in warmup at about 23:34 UTC after the compute-process
gate saw PID 1557838 (`s8-narrow/build/vgpu_bench`) while the capture held
the shared lock. It had not started timing and produced no report.
The sequence stopped there. The remaining process slots are missing.

We applied the shared dispersion implementation in `3188410` through the
published `f80fbb9` helpers. Each within-process percentage is the share
of points over the stated threshold. The cross-process column reports
the corresponding share among the available repeats.

| Mode | Completed / planned | Shortest launch (ms) | IQR / median >10%, by repeat | Max / min >1.5, by repeat | Median range >10% |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Contiguous MLP | 2 / 5 | 1.071 | 13.54%, 12.50% | 7.29%, 6.25% | 2 / 96 (2.08%) |
| Strided MLP | 2 / 5 | 1.243 | 10.00%, 5.00% | 10.00%, 5.00% | 1 / 40 (2.50%) |
| DRAM active-SM read | 2 / 5 | 1.263 | 16.67%, 16.67% | 33.33%, 33.33% | 1 / 6 (16.67%) |
| L2 active-SM read | 1 / 5 | 1.645 | 0.00% | 0.00% | One repeat |

The batch fails the published limits of 5%, 15% and 5%, respectively.
Contiguous and strided MLP fail the IQR rule. DRAM active-SM fails all
three rules on the available data. The L2 active-SM process passes both
within-process rules and has no cross-process estimate. The capture
therefore supplies diagnostic evidence only. The installed GB10 MLP
measurements retain their provisional status.

For comparison with the frozen reference, the partial capture's
256 and 384 KiB `cp_async` DRAM medians are 244.659 and 247.178 GB/s
(reference 227.803 and 241.015 GB/s). The 48-SM DRAM read is 246.521 GB/s
(reference 257.738 GB/s), and the one 48-SM L2 read is 2113.022 GB/s
(reference 2156.177 GB/s). These comparisons use all completed reports
in each mode and are descriptive.
