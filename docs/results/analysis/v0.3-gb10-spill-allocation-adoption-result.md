Status: result of the adoption check registered in [the allocation design](v0.3-spill-allocation-design-registration.md), section "Adoption check", which also decides [the serial 1 KiB exception](v0.3-gb10-spill-serial-boundary-exception-registration.md). **Verdict: not adopted. Both registrations are withdrawn, and `spill-latency` stays as it is.** As registered, N is not changed and the check is not repeated.

## Result under the registered protocol

All seven rounds (5 plus the protocol's 2 extra) are **void**, each for the same reason: the frozen-schedule check (`long_launch.verify`) found a real launch under 1.0 ms. The capture ended `failed`: "modes short of 5 valid rounds after 2 extra rounds". No round was valid, so rules 1 to 3 did not judge any round and the reduce refused to run.

The short launches all come from one point: `serial_chain`, 1 KiB frames, 128 reloads, one of the four points the exception removes from rules 1 and 3. The design applies the 1.0 ms rule to every real launch, so the exception does not cover it.

| Round | Launches of that point under 1.0 ms (of 225) | Shortest launch (us) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 106 | 768 |
| 2 | 106 | 767 |
| 3 | 92 | 748 |
| 4 | 96 | 742 |
| 5 | 103 | 740 |
| 6 | 116 | 758 |
| 7 | 113 | 748 |

No other point had a launch under 1.0 ms.

**Why it happened.** The pilot reads each point in one allocation and grows the steps until its fastest launch reaches 1.25 ms. For this point the pilot's three launches at 128 steps took 1.93 to 1.96 ms, so the point was frozen at 128 steps. In the capture's allocations the same point also took a state about 2.5 times faster (per-allocation medians 0.90 to 1.92 ms, launches down to 0.74 ms). This is the two-state behavior that the exception registers. The pilot saw only its slow state, and the frozen length leaves the fast state under 1.0 ms. Neither registration anticipated this interaction.
