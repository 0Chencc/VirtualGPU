# Post-hoc analysis: GB10 GEMM hypotheses H3 and H4

**Post-hoc.** Everything here uses the opened v0.2 GB10 GEMM holdout labels ([validation/v02-holdout-scores.json](../../../validation/v02-holdout-scores.json)). It cannot change the v0.2 decisions and it does not choose any model structure. It locates where the model's error sits for [v0.2-posthoc.md](v0.2-posthoc.md) H3 and H4, and fixes the microbenchmarks that must decide any change. Structure is chosen on those microbenchmarks only.

## Data and method

- Predictions and KernelIR: the formal v0.2 prediction archive (`predictions.json` SHA-256 `74caeeeb...`) and its regenerated IR.
- Core: the `v0.2-final` wheel (`build_info` formal at `e70824b`). The core at `78c664e` (main) gives identical predictions on every input below; between the two only comments changed.
- Profiles: `v0.2-final` GB10 (`0.21.0-posthoc`, SHA-256 `6f4c9340...`, the frozen one) and main (`0.22.0-posthoc`, five-process MLP medians).
- Counterfactuals change one profile or IR field, nominal profile, no draws. Unchanged, they reproduce the archived nominal predictions.
- Driver: [scripts/posthoc_gb10_h3_h4.py](../../../scripts/posthoc_gb10_h3_h4.py). Outputs: [counterfactuals-profile-0.21.0-posthoc.json](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/counterfactuals-profile-0.21.0-posthoc.json) and [counterfactuals-profile-0.22.0-posthoc.json](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/counterfactuals-profile-0.22.0-posthoc.json).

## H3: `prefill-896` `num_stages = 4` configs predicted 50% to 57% fast

### Confirmed

1. **The miss is DRAM traffic from L2 reuse across waves, not the pipeline.** `prefill-896` is M = 896, N = 6144, K = 3328 with `GROUP_M = 1`, so B is 41 MB against a 24 MB L2 and each wave covers `ctas_per_sm * 48 / tiles_n` rows of M. The `s4` configs fit fewer CTAs per SM, so a wave covers fewer rows and B is read from DRAM more times. If each wave reads its unique A and B panels once, with LRU at wave grain, `m128n128k64-s4` (1 CTA per SM) moves 292 MB, a floor of 1131 us at 258 GB/s; it measures 1185 us cold. The fastest config (`m128n128k64-w8-s1`, 2 CTAs per SM) moves 170 MB, a floor of 656 us; it measures 775 us. The model's DRAM bytes are 95 to 112 MB for the `s4` configs and 100 MB for the fastest.
2. **Mechanism.** Truncating the grid of `m128n128k64-w8-s4` (cold) to 48, 96, 144, 192 and 336 CTAs gives 43, 60, 71, 82 and 112 MB of DRAM: the first wave is right (B plus A), each later wave adds 11 to 17 MB instead of about 42 MB. In the L2 model, `end_tile` advances the stream clock by a chunk's full K stream only when the chunk's last tile ends, and a later read hits with the rescan curve at the clock difference. The 48 B panels of a wave end in the same event, so the clock steps through them in processing order and panel j gets a reuse distance of 41.7 MB minus (j + 1) times 0.87 MB. About 31 of the 48 fall below the GB10 rescan cliff (near 27 MB) and are charged as full hits: 65% of the next wave's B, matching the 27 of 41 MB the grid truncation shows. On hardware those panels stream in step along K, so LRU holds only every panel's last K slices when the next wave starts at K = 0. The profile's own rescan curve is below 0.03 above 1.125 times L2.
3. **Counterfactual.** Setting every `l2_rescan_hit_rate_by_working_set` point to zero removes cross-wave hits (for this case the warm hit is the curve at the 47 MB footprint, 0.005, so warm and cold are both clean):

| Case, cache | Profile | Fastest config's rank | Kendall tau-b | Top-5 regret |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `prefill-896` warm | `0.21.0-posthoc` | 6 to 1 | 0.698 to 0.790 | 5.27% to 0.00% |
| `prefill-896` cold | `0.21.0-posthoc` | 6 to 1 | 0.705 to 0.786 | 1.89% to 0.00% |
| `prefill-896` warm | `0.22.0-posthoc` | 5 to 1 | 0.681 to 0.861 | 0.00% to 0.00% |
| `prefill-1792` warm | `0.21.0-posthoc` | 3 to 3 | 0.683 to 0.771 | 0.00% to 0.00% |

   The three `s4` configs go from -50% to -54% error to -4% to +0.4%. This is a locating tool, not a fix: zeroing the curve also removes legitimate cross-wave reuse, and `mask-mnk-641` drops from tau 0.81 to 0.72.
4. **The pipeline itself does not explain it.** Setting the `s4` configs' prefetch distance to 0 slows them by 28% (709 against 555 us for `m128n128k64-w8-s4`), far short of the 1185 us measured. Halving the MMA rate, dividing the shared memory bandwidth by three, or tripling the `ldmatrix` bytes shifts all configs alike and does not reorder `s4` against `s1`.

### What decides it

The `wave-rescan` microbenchmark (below) measures how much of a re-reading wave hits L2 for GEMM-shaped B streams. The candidate forms use only existing profile fields and have no free parameter:

- **a** (current core): end-order stream clock, predicted by the core on the synthetic KernelIR.
- **b**: chunks that end in the same event record the stream clock from before the event, and the clock then advances past all of them, so a re-read sees the whole wave footprint: `l2_rescan_hit_rate_by_working_set` at that footprint. (Recording the clock from after the event would make every difference zero, and `max(full_stream)` would then charge all 48 panels as hits.)
- **c**: LRU at K-slice grain with capacity `l2_effective_capacity_bytes`: a forward re-read hits fully if the wave fits and not at all otherwise; a reversed re-read hits `min(1, capacity / footprint)`.

At 2 and 3 times L2, form a predicts 70% and 50% hits, forms b and c predict none. Reversed means the K direction: even waves walk K up, odd waves walk it down, so an odd wave starts on the slices the previous wave touched last. This separates b from c (b stays near zero, c gives 50% and 33%); reversing only the panel order would not. KernelIR cannot express a reversed walk, so form a is not defined there.

The measured hit fraction of a re-reading wave interpolates its time per byte between the first wave of the same launch shape (all misses) and the 0.5 times L2 point of the same CTAs per SM and rows per wave (all hits). The first-wave baseline is a one-wave launch in the same K direction (up for forward points, down for reversed ones), so a direction-dependent prefetch effect cancels; the one-wave launches are baselines only and do not score forms a, b or c. Near the cliff form c is a step (1 at or below `l2_effective_capacity_bytes`, 0 above); it is reported as frozen, with no smoothing, and no capacity is inferred from these data.

## H4: `resident-wave` (M = 48), top 8 within 3.6%

### Confirmed

5. **Two errors of opposite sign straddle the DRAM ceiling.** B is 66 MB, read once. The fastest group (`s4` and `s1` with 2 CTAs per SM) measures 255.8 us cold, 258.3 GB/s, equal to `dram_bw_read_gbps`; the model calls them DRAM-latency bound at 266 to 271 us (+4% to +5%). The `s2` group (prefetch distance 0, 3 to 9 CTAs per SM) is called DRAM-bandwidth bound at the ceiling, 262.3 us, and measures 270 to 273 us.
6. **Located on profile `0.21.0-posthoc`.** Scaling only the three `cp_async` MLP points from 512 KiB to 1.5 MiB (258.3, 257.4, 257.4 GB/s) by 1.05 takes `resident-wave-exact` cold from tau 0.309 and regret 5.52% to 0.598 and 0.39%; scaling the points below 512 KiB, at 2.3 MiB, or from 4.6 MiB does nothing. Scaling the whole curve by 1.02 is enough (0.738, 0.39%); making it monotone non-decreasing gets most of it (0.608, 0.39%). The closed loop of these configs sits on that part of the curve and delivers 2% to 3% below the bandwidth path's ceiling (`dram_bw_by_active_sms`, 257.7 GB/s at 48 SMs).
7. **On profile `0.22.0-posthoc` the case already scores well, by accident.** Its MLP plateau (259 to 263 GB/s) lies above the unchanged `dram_bw_by_active_sms` (257.7), so the latency path no longer falls below the ceiling: `resident-wave-exact` cold tau 0.741, regret 0.39%, and none of the scalings in item 6 move it. The +4% to +5% latency-bound charge is gone: the measured top 8 are all predicted at 262.3 us, the active-SM ceiling (errors +0.1% to +2.5%), so the model ties them, and the `s2` group is still about 3% fast. In `0.22.0` the read ceiling 263.55 is the largest five-run MLP median (`ld_global_v4`, 1.5 MiB), not an independent probe, and `dram_bw_by_active_sms` and both closed-loop tables are unchanged from calibration run `cal-20261125T102038Z-sm_121`, so the curve and the ceiling still come from different sessions.
8. **The DRAM rate differs by session.** In [gb10-memory-repeat-posthoc.json](gb10-memory-repeat-posthoc.json) (five repeats on Spark 1), every DRAM closed-loop and MLP point at 384 KiB or more lies between 239 and 244 GB/s (spread under 1.5%). The `0.21.0` single runs are 245 to 259, the `0.22.0` medians 259 to 263, the GEMM reached 258 during the holdout capture, and the independent read probe of the `wave-rescan` smoke run (2026-11-26 20:46 UTC) gave 247.8 before and after. The model combines curves and ceilings from different sessions in absolute GB/s, and a 2% disagreement between them decides this case.

### What decides it

The `b-stream` microbenchmark measures M = 48-shaped B streams at DRAM. Every block of points is bracketed by an independent full-device read probe (`ld.global.cg` over 256 MiB after a clean flush, with its samples and UTC time), never the MLP maximum. Two errors are reported for every point: against the frozen profile in absolute bandwidth and time, and as a fraction of the block's probe, ratios above 1 kept. The first carries the session drift, which is the real floor for absolute predictions; the second isolates the shape of the curve and the latency or bandwidth classification. The questions are whether prefetch distance 1 or 2 with 2 CTAs per SM reaches the probe (on the GEMM, `0.21.0` gives 97% to 98% of the profile ceiling and `0.22.0` gives 100%), and whether prefetch distance 0 with 3 to 9 CTAs per SM stays about 5% below it (both profiles give 100%). Access pattern (128 or 256 byte rows against linear, which changes sector use and DRAM row-buffer hits) and the prefetch distance 0 phase effect are separate factors of the grid and are not merged into one term. A profile change is discussed only after the drift is shown to repeat across sessions, and then as an optional per-block reference value next to the absolute fields, not as a conversion of existing fields into ratios; errors of values that share one reference are treated as correlated.

Each mode samples 15 times per point, and every timed `b-stream` launch follows a read-only flush of 128 MiB. Every kernel runs at least one MMA per stage, since the core models only kernels with MMA work. Occupancy is fixed by padding shared memory, recorded per point, and `ptxas` reports no spills for any instantiation. Captures run behind the GPU lock and the GB10 thermal gate, with telemetry every 0.5 s and a check that no large CPU job shares the unified memory. The 0.5 times L2 single waves last about 50 us, so launch overhead is a visible share of them.

A three-sample smoke run of `wave-rescan` on GB10 showed that the first version reversed K per pass, so with two rows per wave the second row of every wave was reversed too. The reversal now applies to whole odd waves. No frozen prediction depends on it: forms b and c are analytic and form a is not defined for reversed points.

## Results

Captured on Spark 1 at `96646c0` (binary SHA-256 `9ff6c14e...`), 2026-11-26 20:51 to 20:56 UTC, 15 samples per point, GPU lock held, no other compute process, thermal gate passed before each mode (52 to 54 C, SM clock 2489 to 2515 MHz). Raw captures with telemetry: [captures/](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/captures/); scores against the frozen predictions: [scores.json](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/scores.json), from [microbench/posthoc_gb10_score.py](../../../microbench/posthoc_gb10_score.py). Forms a, b and c are scored as frozen, with no adjustment.

### `wave-rescan` (H3)

Mean absolute error of the predicted hit fraction of a re-reading wave, over the 96 points that are not the 0.5 times L2 references (48 forward, 48 reversed), profile `0.22.0-posthoc` (`0.21.0` gives the same to three decimals):

| Form | Forward, all | Forward, 1.25 to 3 times L2 | Reversed, all | Reversed, 1.25 to 3 times L2 | Near the cliff (1.0 to 1.12 times), forward / reversed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a (current core) | 0.436 | 0.691 | not defined | not defined | 0.322 / not defined |
| b | 0.047 | 0.034 | 0.360 | 0.553 | 0.078 / 0.324 |
| c | 0.085 | 0.037 | 0.046 | 0.065 | 0.226 / 0.044 |

- Form a is rejected: above 1.25 times L2 a forward re-read hits 0.00 to 0.14 on hardware against 0.47 to 0.91 predicted, at every occupancy and rows per wave.
- Form b is rejected by the reversed points: odd waves walking K down hit 0.25 to 0.91 where b predicts 0.01 or less.
- Form c is the only one consistent with both directions. Reversed, it predicts 0.80, 0.67, 0.50, 0.33 at 1.25, 1.5, 2 and 3 times L2 against 0.69 to 0.91, 0.57 to 0.77, 0.37 to 0.61 and 0.25 to 0.38 measured.
- Its known deviation is the cliff: c is a step at `l2_effective_capacity_bytes`, the hardware a slope (forward 0.89 to 0.99 at 1.0 times L2, 0.13 to 0.60 at 1.09 to 1.12 times). It is reported as frozen.
- Two rows per wave hit 0.04 to 0.14 forward above 1.25 times L2, a little more than one row (0.00 to 0.02).

### `staged-mma` (H3 as stated, negative control)

Over 40 points the model's stage turnaround has a mean absolute error of 5.4% (median 4.2%, at most 15.4%), the same with both profiles. In the `prefill-896` `s4` regime (1 CTA per SM, 3 buffers, 32 KiB stages, 0.4 to 1.6 us of MMA per stage) it is -5.1% to -1.3%. Multi-buffer pipelines with MMA are not mispredicted on GB10, so H3 as stated is rejected; the `s4` miss is the cross-wave L2 reuse of item 2. The largest errors are stages with one MMA iteration, bound by the L2 port, predicted 13% to 15% fast.

### `b-stream` (H4)

- **The read probe is not a ceiling.** The `ld.global.cg` probe moved between 226 and 249 GB/s within five minutes (one block: 246 before, 226 after), and the streams beat it at most points (fraction of probe up to 1.09). Normalizing by it adds its own noise, so the per-block fractions are recorded but not used for conclusions. A saturating probe is needed before any per-block reference value is considered.
- **Absolute errors against the frozen profiles:** median 4.1% (`0.21.0`) and 5.1% (`0.22.0`), mean 5.3% and 5.8%, almost all with the model fast. The session's best streams reach 250 to 256 GB/s against the profiles' 257.7 cap, and most points lie at 235 to 250.
- **Prefetch distance 0 with 3 to 9 CTAs per SM does not lose 5%.** The median bandwidth over points with 3 or more CTAs per SM is 243.5, 244.8 and 244.5 GB/s for 1, 2 and 3 buffers. Prefetch distance 0 only falls behind where the bytes in flight are small (1 or 2 CTAs per SM with 4 to 8 KiB stages: 165 to 235 against 221 to 249). The `s2` deficit of the `resident-wave` GEMMs is therefore not reproduced by the closed loop.
- **The access pattern matters as much as the loop.** Median over all points: linear 248.5, 256-byte rows 245.1, 128-byte rows 239.1 GB/s. The core has no term for it.
- Pipelined loops at 2 CTAs per SM (8 to 16 KiB) reach 246 to 256 GB/s, at the top of the session, as the fastest `resident-wave` GEMMs do.

H4 is not located in the closed loop. What remains between the `resident-wave` groups (3% to 5%) is the size of the access-pattern effect and of the session drift, and the GEMM's masked 48-row A loads are not in `b-stream`.

### Dispersion check on the GB10 captures (added after capture)

The capture gate added at `73f7079` voids a capture when more than 10% of its sample lists spread by more than 1.3 times (max over min). Applied afterwards to the GB10 captures above: `b-stream` passes (4.3%) and `staged-mma` passes (2.5%), but `wave-rescan` fails (16.2%). Its spread is a tail, not two modes: in most flagged lists 12 to 14 of 15 samples agree within a few percent and one to three are slower, in launches of 50 to 300 us.

A replication with the gate ([captures-gb10-wave-rescan-replication/](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/captures-gb10-wave-rescan-replication/), 2026-11-26 22:33 UTC, same binary and frozen predictions) also fails (14.0%). Its scores, for the record: form a 0.468 forward; form b 0.142 forward and 0.372 reversed; form c 0.179 forward and 0.039 reversed. Point by point the two runs' hit fractions differ by 0.023 at the median and 0.507 at most. Under the gate neither `wave-rescan` run counts; how to capture short launches so that they pass is open.

### Status under the adopted capture rules (2026-11-27)

The single max-over-min gate above was replaced the same day by three rules fixed in advance after an independent audit, applied to timing and bandwidth sample lists only: (1) at most 5% of lists may have an interquartile range above 10% of their median; (2) at most 15% may have a max over min above 1.5; (3) across runs of one geometry, at most 5% of points may have per-run medians spanning more than 10% of their median. ## `wave-rescan-long` (v2), frozen predictions

Frozen before any capture: [predictions-v0.2-final-core.json](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/wave-rescan-long/predictions-v0.2-final-core.json) (form a, the `v0.2-final` core) and [predictions-current-core.json](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/wave-rescan-long/predictions-current-core.json) (the current core, which equals the analytic form c on every forward point, and the analytic forms b and c), both on GB10 profile `0.22.0-posthoc`. All are steady-state hit fractions of a re-reading wave: forward, every wave walks K up and starts on what LRU kept from the last one, as the second wave of `wave-rescan` did; reversed, the walk alternates every wave, so each wave starts on the slices the previous one touched last. The forms' values are those frozen for `wave-rescan`. The capture runs `wave-rescan-long` with `--l2-gbps 2276.489` (that profile's `l2_bw_read_gbps`), twice; both runs must pass the three capture rules, and the forms are scored by the mean absolute error of the hit fraction, as before.

### `wave-rescan-long` capture: void under rule 3

## Registration: qualifying forward capture (`wave-rescan-v3`)

Registered before any capture of this mode, for review; it uses only microbenchmarks.

- **Question.** Does a forward re-read of a wave larger than the effective L2 capacity hit? This is the part of the cross-wave change that real kernels use.
- **Kernel.** `wave-rescan-long` with the forward walk and its forward no-reuse reference only, footprints 0.5, 0.75, 1.25, 1.5, 2 and 3 times L2, at 1, 2 and 4 CTAs per SM and 1 or 2 rows per wave. The band from 0.85 to 1.15 times L2 is excluded because the `wave-rescan-long` capture showed its steady state flipping between runs; this exclusion is a protocol choice for this new capture only and is not applied to any earlier data. Launch shapes, wave counts, flushes and samples are those of `wave-rescan-long`, `--l2-gbps 2276.489`.
- **Predictions.** The frozen `wave-rescan-long` files ([v0.2-final core](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/wave-rescan-long/predictions-v0.2-final-core.json), [current core](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/wave-rescan-long/predictions-current-core.json)) already hold every point of this grid; nothing is recomputed. Two candidates: **a**, the `v0.2-final` per-chunk clock; and **no hit above capacity**, which forms b and c both predict for forward walks (c: 0; b: 0.01 or less).
- **Runs and rules.** At least three runs, each a separate process under the GPU lock and the thermal gate. The capture qualifies only if every run passes rules 1 and 2 and the runs together pass rule 3, all on this grid.
- **Decision.** Over the 24 points at 1.25 to 3 times L2, the measured hit fraction of each run (between the no-reuse reference and the 0.5 times L2 point, as in `wave-rescan-long`) is compared with each candidate by mean absolute error. A candidate is supported if its error is the lower one in every run; if the runs disagree, the result is inconclusive. If the capture does not qualify, the run-to-run instability is reported as the result and the cross-wave change rests on consistent but unqualified evidence.

## Decision on the cross-wave change

`wave-rescan-v3` is judged as registered: the capture does not qualify, and the run-to-run instability is the result. The change stays in the core, for these reasons:

1. In all seven runs of `wave-rescan`, `wave-rescan-long` and `wave-rescan-v3`, forward re-reads at 1.5 times L2 or more hit -0.01 to 0.09, while the earlier per-chunk clock predicts 0.5 to 0.9 and is wrong in every run. This part is stable across runs; reverting would bring back the form that is further off in all of them.
2. No capture passes rule 3 because the GB10 steady state is bistable from about 0.75 to 1.25 times L2 (each run internally consistent, the host quiet in the traced run). That is hardware behavior, not noise that more captures remove ([observation](v0.3-gb10-l2-bistability-observation.md)).
3. The change therefore rests on GB10 microbenchmarks that agree in direction but do not pass the capture rules: stable support at 1.5 times L2 or more, a step against bistable hardware from about 0.75 to 1.25 times L2 (known limit), unvalidated on the RTX 4070. Whether it counts is decided by the sealed v0.3 holdout, not by these microbenchmarks.

**Stable part, post-hoc.** Restricted after the fact to points at 1.5 times L2 or more (not a registered decision; [stable-subset-rules-posthoc.json](v0.3-gb10-h3-h4/stable-subset-rules-posthoc.json)): forward points pass all three rules in every version (rule 3: 0% in `wave-rescan`, 1.4% in `wave-rescan-long`, 0% in `wave-rescan-v3`; rules 1 and 2 at most 0% and 1.4% per run). With the reversed partial-hit points of `wave-rescan-long` included, rule 3 is 5.6% and fails.

`wave-rescan` is closed; no fourth version is planned. A dedicated experiment on the bistable band and the reversed-walk detail are listed for after v0.3.
