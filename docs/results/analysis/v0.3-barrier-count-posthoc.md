# Post-hoc: does the number of barriers per loop step belong in the core?

**Post-hoc.** Uses only the opened v0.2 holdout labels and the archived v0.2 IRs; no core change, no unopened data. It answers one question from `ROADMAP.md`: the core charges one stage synchronization (`sync_ns`) per pipeline step whatever the number of barriers in the loop, while the v0.2 kernels have 2 to 14 per step. Is the barrier count worth a mechanism candidate after v0.3?

## What the profile measures

`fixed_sync_ns` comes from `fixed_sync_empty_kernel` (`m2_kernels.cuh`): per step, one `cp.async.wait_group`, one `__syncthreads()` and one `cp.async.commit_group`, with no other work. `stage_sync_overhead_ns` comes from the `stage_overhead_*` kernels, also one `__syncthreads()` per step. So the profile value is the cost of one barrier per step, and the core charges that one value per step (`sim.rs`, `Phase::Main`), whatever the loop's barrier op says.

## Data

- Labels: the measured medians of the opened v0.2 holdouts, [v02-holdout-scores.json](../../../validation/v02-holdout-scores.json) (sha256 `6557eebc...`), 1,024 measured configurations (GEMM 324 and attention 188 per GPU), warm and cold.
- Predictions: the current core (built at `ad2fc38`; no core change up to `d8eefdb`) on the bundled `gb10.json` and `rtx4070.json`, nominal (`draws=0`), from the archived v0.2 holdout IRs (1,104 files, digest `28f17ea3...` of the sorted `sha256sum` list; not in the repository). Signed error is `predicted / measured - 1`; negative means predicted too fast.
- The v0.2 development set is not included: its IR archive is not on the machine this was run on.

## Barrier counts

From the IRs' loop barrier op (the PTX barriers per step the frontend counts):

| | 2 per step | 3 | 10 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GB10 GEMM | 324 | | | |
| RTX 4070 GEMM | 156 | 168 | | |
| Attention, each GPU | 144 | | 34 | 10 |

They are not spread over the configurations. In attention, 10 and 14 occur only with head dimension 128 and 64-row tiles; head dimension 128 with 128-row tiles, and every head dimension 64 tile, has 2. Within the 64-row, head dimension 128 configurations, 14 occurs only at 8 warps (`m64n64`, `m64n128`), 10 at 4 warps and at 8 warps with `m64n32`. In RTX 4070 GEMM, 3 tracks Triton's stage count (most `s2` and all `s4` configurations); on GB10 the same configurations compile to 2.

## Residuals by barrier count

Median signed error, warm / cold:

| GPU | Attention, 2 | 10 | 14 | GEMM, 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GB10 | -4.7% / +2.5% | -7.4% / -7.1% | -13.2% / -12.9% | -8.3% / -7.6% | |
| RTX 4070 | +5.6% / +7.1% | -4.4% / -3.8% | -11.2% / -8.4% | -4.0% / -1.1% | -1.9% / -0.4% |

In attention the error falls with the barrier count on both GPUs. What can and cannot be separated:

- **10 against 14: no barrier effect left at a fixed warp count.** At 8 warps, 10 barriers (`m64n32`) and 14 (`m64n64`) give -14.6% and -15.0% on GB10 (warm) and -12.9% and -11.2% on the RTX 4070; at 4 warps, 10 barriers give -7.5% and -1.3% (`m64n32`, `m64n64`, GB10). The step from 10 to 14 follows the warp count, not the barriers.
- **2 against 10: not separable.** The change from 2 to 10 comes with a change of head dimension (64 to 128 at 64-row tiles) or of tile height (128 to 64 rows at head dimension 128). Along the first, GB10 warm does not move (-8.0% to -7.4%) while GB10 cold and the RTX 4070 fall by 9 to 11 points; along the second, all four fall by 12 to 15 points. Head dimension and tile shape could carry all of it.
- **GEMM, 2 against 3 (RTX 4070), goes the other way:** at Triton `s2` the 2-barrier configurations are predicted -5.7% (warm) and the 3-barrier ones +0.2%. These are different tiles (the 2-barrier `s2` configurations are 24 of 108), so this is not a barrier effect either.
- The `sync` share of the predicted time (`bound_fraction`) is 0 in every configuration, so the per-step charge never limits a v0.2 prediction outright, though it does enter the time.

## A counterfactual with no fitted parameter

Charging the measured one-barrier cost once per barrier (each profile's `fixed_sync_ns` multiplied by the configuration's barrier count, core otherwise unchanged) narrows the attention groups' medians (GB10 warm -2.1%, +2.8%, -3.9%; RTX 4070 warm +7.5%, +2.9%, +2.3%), but over all configurations it does not help:

| Cohort, warm | Median absolute error | Median Kendall tau per case |
|---|---|---|
| GB10 attention | 10.0% to 9.0% | 0.641 to 0.620 |
| GB10 GEMM | 9.0% to 8.8% | 0.788 to 0.788 |
| RTX 4070 attention | 6.8% to 7.5% | 0.714 to 0.707 |
| RTX 4070 GEMM | 7.4% to 8.9% | 0.803 to 0.787 |

Cold is the same in direction (GB10 attention 8.6% to 8.4%, RTX 4070 GEMM 6.9% to 7.6%; tau within 0.015). These are the opened labels, so this validates nothing either way.

## Answer

Not worth a mechanism candidate on this evidence. At a fixed warp count the barrier count shows no effect (10 against 14); the larger contrast (2 against 10) cannot be told apart from head dimension and tile shape in this set; the RTX 4070 GEMM contrast points the other way; and charging each barrier with the measured cost makes absolute error and ranking no better overall. If the question comes back, the first step is a microbenchmark that varies only the barriers per step at fixed work, not more mining of these residuals.

The two `ROADMAP.md` items stay open as known model behaviour; this note settles neither.
